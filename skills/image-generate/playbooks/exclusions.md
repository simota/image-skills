# Saying what must not be there

Exclusions are the half of a request people skip, and they are the half a
reviewer can actually check. "Warm and human" cannot be failed. "No handshakes,
no glass office towers" can.

## Name things, not feelings

A negative that names a feeling is a second positive instruction wearing a
minus sign, and it tends to be read as a topic to include.

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| not corporate | no suits, no boardrooms, no glass towers, no handshakes |
| not cluttered | at most three objects in frame |
| not cartoonish | no outlines, no exaggerated proportions, no flat fills |
| not dark | nothing below mid-grey in the lower half |
| avoid AI look | no extra fingers, no warped text, no symmetric face |

The right-hand column shares one property: someone else can open the file and
say whether it holds.

## Two kinds, and they go in different places

**Content exclusions** are about what appears — objects, people, text,
branding. These come from the brief's `excludes` and travel verbatim.

**Artefact exclusions** are about how the generation typically fails —
watermarks, garbled lettering, duplicated limbs, a second horizon. These are
generic, they are the same on every request, and they belong on the `Avoid:`
line as a standing block rather than being reinvented each time.

Keeping them apart matters because the first is a decision that can be wrong
and the second is boilerplate that can be reused.

## The standing block

Most requests want some of these, and the ones that do not are usually asking
for exactly the thing being excluded:

```text
Avoid: watermarks, signatures, logos, borders, frames, captions,
       any text not specified above, extra limbs or digits, duplicated subjects
```

Delete a line when the request wants it — a poster wants text, a product shot
may want a logo. Deleting deliberately is the point; carrying the block
unexamined is how a request forbids the thing it was for.

## What an exclusion cannot do

Exclusions constrain; they do not compose. A request made entirely of negatives
gets the average of everything not forbidden, which is the same average as no
request at all. If the exclusion list is longer than the positive description,
the direction is unfinished and this is the wrong stage to be in.

## For an edit, exclusions become invariants

*Change only X, keep Y unchanged* — restated in every iteration, not only the
first. Drift is cumulative and invisible one step at a time, and by the fourth
iteration a face that was never supposed to change has changed.

## Checking the list

- Does every entry name something a person could point at in the result?
- Did every `excludes` entry from the brief survive into the request, verbatim?
- Is anything on the list actually wanted? Read it once as an instruction to a
  literal-minded stranger, which is what it is
