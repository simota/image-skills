# Picking the keeper

The candidate that catches the eye first is the one that is most unusual, and
unusual is not the same as right. Selection is done in a fixed order so that the
first impression is data rather than the verdict.

## The order

1. **Open all of them.** Every candidate, individually, at full size. This is
   not a formality — a decision made from a grid of thumbnails is a decision
   about thumbnails
2. **Write the first impression of each, in one line, before comparing.** Cold,
   without the brief in front of you
3. **Now read the brief's axes.** Score each candidate against each axis: holds,
   partly, fails. Not a number — a word, per axis
4. **Eliminate on hard axes.** An axis the brief made non-negotiable — the empty
   upper third, the absent text, the crop that must survive — eliminates,
   whatever the picture looks like
5. **Compare what survives on the soft axes.** Usually two candidates
6. **Name the axis that decided it.** Not an adjective

## What eliminates regardless of beauty

- Rendered text that is wrong, if text was asked for
- A defect at the shipping size — not one visible only at 100%
- Anything on the exclusion list, present
- A crop the brief said must survive, that does not
- Anything that depicts a real person, product or organisation when the brief
  did not ask for it

## The traps

**The striking one.** The candidate that stands out in a set of four is often
the one that departed most from the request. Check its axes first, not last.

**The nearly.** A candidate that is right except for one fixable flaw beats a
candidate that is uniformly acceptable — *if* the flaw is genuinely local. Read
`edit-ladder` before assuming it is.

**The grid.** Judging from a contact sheet selects for silhouette and
contrast, which is a real property of an image and almost never the one the
brief was about.

**The sunk batch.** Four disappointing candidates do not become better by being
compared with each other. If none clears the hard axes, the answer is none.

## Writing it down

```
kept:      candidate 3
because:   the only one whose upper third stays empty at the 1:1 crop (axis 2)
runner-up: candidate 1 — better light, fails axis 2
rejected:  2 (garbled text), 4 (person in frame, excluded)
```

**The rejected line is worth more than the kept line** to whoever comes next: it
says what was already tried and what it cost.
