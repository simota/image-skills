# Deciding what is in frame

A generator will invent a subject if one is not given, and it will compose
around whatever it invented. Both decisions are cheaper to make than to undo.

## Decide in this order

Later items depend on earlier ones. Deciding the crop before the subject means
choosing a frame for something that does not exist yet.

1. **Subject** — the one thing the picture is of, in a noun phrase. If two
   things compete for this, the picture has two subjects and will read as
   having none
2. **What the subject is doing** — a state or an action. A subject with no verb
   comes back as a catalogue shot
3. **Setting** — where it is. "No setting" is a decision too, and it is called
   a plain backdrop
4. **Framing** — how close, from what height, where the subject sits in frame
5. **Light** — direction, hardness, time of day. This is the single strongest
   lever over mood, and the one most often left to chance
6. **Negative space** — what the picture leaves empty, and for what

## Negative space is a requirement, not a leftover

If text, a logo, or a UI element lands on this picture, the space it needs is
part of the subject decision — not something to hope for and crop later. Say
where it goes and how much of the frame it takes.

A hero with a headline over it is not "a picture with room"; it is a picture
whose upper third is deliberately empty and whose focal point sits low right.

## Every crop the picture must survive

One image, several containers, and the container that clips it is not the one
it was composed for. List them:

```
composed for:  16:9, focal point at lower right third
must survive:  1:1 centre crop (mobile), 2:1 top crop (social card)
```

Then either the composition satisfies all of them, or the brief says a second
picture is needed. **Discovering this at export is discovering it too late** —
that stage owns files, and it cannot recompose a photograph.

## The tests a subject decision has to pass

- Can it be said in one noun phrase? If not, it is a scene, and scenes drift
- Would two people reading it draw the same thing? Where they would not, the
  gap is the decision that has not been made
- Does the verb survive translation into a still frame? "Collaborating" does
  not; "leaning over a shared screen" does
- Is anything in it a real person, a real product, or a real place? Then it
  stops here and goes back to the human

## What this produces

Six lines — subject, action, setting, framing, light, negative space — and a
crop list. That is enough for someone else to write the request, which is the
next stage's job and not this one's.
