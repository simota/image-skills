# Locate the fault before changing anything

"It's not quite right" is the most expensive sentence in this family. Acted on
directly it becomes a re-roll — a different picture, arriving with a different
set of problems, and no way to tell whether anything improved.

## Turn the complaint into a location

Every change starts from a fault that can be pointed at. Three things make one:

1. **Where** — a region, in words another reader can find. "Lower left third",
   "the subject's right hand", "the horizon behind the building"
2. **What** — the observable wrongness. Not "ugly": six fingers, a doubled
   horizon, letters that are not letters, a shadow falling the wrong way
3. **Against what** — the axis or invariant it violates. Without this, a fault
   is a preference, and preferences reopen forever

```
where:    upper right quadrant, the window
what:     the reflection shows a second light source
against:  brief axis 3 — one light source
```

## When the complaint has no location

Two possibilities, and they lead in opposite directions.

**The target is global.** A settled whole-frame correction (such as the agreed
palette and lighting) is an edit objective; name the whole frame and the
invariants. If the desired look itself is undecided, return to direction.
Global scope alone does not prove that the request was wrong.

**The fault has not been looked for yet.** Go back and look. Full size,
quadrant by quadrant, then at the shipping size. Most "not quite right" is one
locatable thing, and the rest is usually two.

## Invariants come before the change

Everything that must not move is written down first — subject, crop, palette,
light direction, the parts already approved. Then it is restated in *every*
iteration, not only the first.

Drift is cumulative and invisible one step at a time. By the fourth iteration a
face that was never supposed to change has changed, and nobody can say which
step did it because no step named it.

```
change only: the reflection in the upper right window
keep:        the desk, the lamp position, the crop, the palette, the subject's face
```

## One falsifiable correction objective per iteration

Group coupled operations when that is the cheaper plausible repair: remove an
object and fill its background, repair both hands, extend while preserving the
subject position, harmonize colour and lighting, or replace repeated artefacts
and several letters in one region. State every affected region and invariant.
Split when independent goals conflict or causal comparison is needed; do not
claim to have isolated which component caused an improvement in a bundled edit.

## Compare, do not admire

After the change, open the before and the after together and answer two
questions in order:

1. Did the located fault go away?
2. Did anything on the invariant list move?

Question two is the one that gets skipped, and it is the one that catches
drift. A result that fixed the fault and moved an invariant is not an
improvement; it is a trade nobody agreed to.

## Stopping

Two attempts at the same fault, the same way, is the limit. A third is spending
budget to prove something already demonstrated. Move up the ladder, or hand it
back with what was tried.
