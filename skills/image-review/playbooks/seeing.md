# An order of looking

The eye goes where the picture sends it, which is exactly where the defects are
not. A review that follows attention finds what the image already handles well.
This is an order that does not.

## Before the image

Do not read the prompt yet. Knowing what was asked for makes a reader see what
was asked for — the most reliable way to miss a subject that is subtly wrong.

## The passes

**1 · Cold, at shipping size, three seconds.** Look, look away, and write one
line: what is this a picture of, and what does it feel like? This is the only
moment the first impression is available, and it is the closest thing to how
the picture will actually be met.

**2 · Full size, quadrant by quadrant.** Divide the frame into four and look at
each on its own terms, ignoring the subject. Most artefacts live where nothing
important is happening, because that is where the model had least to go on.

**3 · The known failure sites.** Hands, eyes, teeth, jewellery, the join
between a limb and a body, text of any kind, repeated patterns, the far side of
a reflection, the line where two materials meet, the horizon behind something.
Go to each deliberately. `defect-catalogue` lists what to expect at each.

**4 · Physics.** One light source or several? Do the shadows agree with it? Do
the reflections show what is actually in front of them? Does the perspective
hold across the frame? These read as "something is off" long before anyone can
name them.

**5 · Text, character by character.** Every glyph, against what was asked for.
Not "the sign says the right thing" — read it letter by letter. This is where
this class of model fails most visibly, and it is the failure a viewer notices
fastest.

**6 · Now read the brief.** Then go back through and check each axis. Off-brief
is a defect of the run and it is ranked with the rest.

**7 · At shipping size again.** Which of what you found is actually visible
here? A defect nobody will see is a note, not a finding — say which findings
exist only at full size.

## Both ends of the scale

An image is reviewed at the size it will be seen and at full size, and the two
answer different questions. Full size finds defects; shipping size decides
whether they matter. Reporting only one is how a review either blocks on
invisible flaws or passes something that falls apart in place.

## For a set

Look at each member in this order first, then look at them together for
consistency of light, palette, scale, and treatment. A set is judged member by
member and again as a set, and the second pass catches what no single image
shows.

## What this produces

A first impression written before the brief was read, a list of located
findings, and a note of what was not looked at — the crops not checked, the
sizes not viewed, the members not opened.
