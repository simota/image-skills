# Ranking what you found

The temptation is to rank by strangeness. Six fingers is *odd*, and oddness
feels like severity. What matters is who sees it, where, and what happens then.

## The question that ranks

**At the size and place this ships, who notices, and what do they conclude?**

| Severity | Test |
|---|---|
| `blocking` | A viewer notices at shipping size and concludes something is wrong — with the picture, the product, or the claim it makes |
| `major` | Noticeable at shipping size, reads as sloppy rather than broken |
| `minor` | Visible on inspection, invisible in place |
| `note` | Correct to record, wrong to act on now |

## What is always blocking

Not because of how it looks — because of what it does.

- **Wrong rendered text**, when text was asked for. It is read, and it is wrong
- **Anything on the exclusion list**, present
- **A hard axis of the brief**, failed
- **A depicted real person, product, or organisation** that the brief did not
  ask for
- **A crop the brief required, that does not survive**

## What is usually over-ranked

- **Artefacts at 100% on a picture that ships at 400px.** Real, and invisible
- **Anatomy in a small or occluded subject.** Judge it at the size it appears
- **Departure from the reference.** The reference was for a principle, not a
  target. Check the brief's `left` line before calling it a defect
- **Preference dressed as craft.** "The composition would be stronger the other
  way round" is direction, not a defect, and it belongs upstream

## What is usually under-ranked

- **Physics that does not add up.** Nobody can name it; everybody feels it, and
  it is the most common cause of "this looks AI-generated"
- **Inconsistency across a set.** Each member passes; the set does not
- **Off-brief but attractive.** The pleasant candidate that answers a different
  question is the hardest defect to hold the line on, and the most expensive to
  discover after it ships

## Each finding carries three things

```
severity: major
where:    upper right window, the reflection
what:     shows a second light source; the scene has one, from the right
```

A finding with no `where` cannot be re-found, and a finding nobody else can
find again is an opinion. Rank it as `note` and say why it could not be
located, rather than promoting it on conviction.

## The verdict

One line, and it commits:

- **usable** — ship it
- **usable with the listed fixes** — name which findings are the fixes
- **not usable** — say what would change that, and which stage owns it

"It depends" is not a verdict. If it genuinely does, the standard was never
settled, and that is the finding.
